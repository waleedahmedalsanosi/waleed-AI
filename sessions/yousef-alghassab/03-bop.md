## The Opportunity in One Paragraph

أمين is a Saudi healthcare review and recommendation platform that connects patients with doctors and facilities through verified, AI-filtered experiences — solving the persistent gap between how Saudis actually find healthcare providers (word of mouth, family networks) and what a structured, credible alternative could look like. The B2B monetization model targets healthcare providers through SaaS subscriptions, with a data analytics layer that gives clinics and hospitals actionable demand intelligence. The product concept is unusually well-scoped for a pre-seed stage: three user roles defined, a legally-defensible Saudi-specific review mechanism (coded doctor profiles), and a clear AI role in maintaining review quality. Dreamy's bet here is early — no traction, unknown founder background — but the co-founding appetite and product clarity make this worth a conditional engagement if the founder delivers on the conditions set in evaluation.

## Product & Technical Fit

**What's being built:** A multi-sided healthcare platform with patient-facing search and verified reviews, a provider-facing dashboard with analytics, and an admin moderation layer — with AI-powered review filtering and a coded-profile system to navigate Saudi legal constraints.

**Build complexity:** Medium-High — three distinct user roles, AI integration for review filtering, verification mechanisms (appointment linkage, location signals), and a legally-sensitive data model all in one MVP

**Dreamy's technical angle:** Mobile-first product (implied), backend for multi-role auth and provider profiles, AI/NLP for review analysis, geolocation, and a data dashboard for providers — full-stack with AI components

**MVP scope estimate:** 6–9 month build for a 2-person Dreamy team (core patient and provider flows; admin panel; AI filtering layer); booking integration and recruitment features explicitly deferred to later phases

## Business Model

**Revenue model:** B2B SaaS subscription for healthcare providers (hospitals, clinics, physiotherapy centres); freemium launch to build supply and demand, then graduated paid tiers based on feature access and analytics depth

**Equity model fit:** Yousef did not reject Waleed's 30% proposal; actively interested in co-founding relationship; new entity recommended to clean up liability — minor friction but not a blocker

**Path to unit economics:** Providers pay for premium analytics, advanced profile features, and eventually recruitment tools; patient side remains free to drive volume; unit economics hinge on provider willingness-to-pay in KSA, which is unvalidated
